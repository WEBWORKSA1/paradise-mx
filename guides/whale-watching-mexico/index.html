Whale watching in Mexico: species, seasons and best spots

Humpbacks, gray whales, blue whales and whale sharks: when and where to see them responsibly.

Paradise.mx editorial team. Reviewed 2026-11-26.

Mexico is one of the best places on Earth to see large marine animals. The warm, sheltered waters of the Pacific coast and the Sea of Cortez are breeding grounds for whales that migrate thousands of kilometres from Alaska and beyond, while the Caribbean has the world's largest fish. Here's when and where to go.

Humpback whales

Humpbacks arrive on Mexico's Pacific coast in winter to mate and give birth. They're the acrobats of the whale world, often breaching, slapping their fins and tails and singing. In Puerto Vallarta and Punta Mita, boats leave daily from the marinas, and you can sometimes see them from shore. In Los Cabos, the official season runs from mid-December to mid-April, and sunset cruises often combine whales with the famous rock arch. Further south, small boats from Puerto Escondido and Mazunte offer quieter encounters.

Gray whales

Gray whales make one of the longest migrations of any mammal, from the Arctic to Baja's lagoons, to calve in calm, shallow water. Famously, mothers sometimes bring their calves right up to small boats. The main lagoons are protected and only licensed local boats may enter. La Paz and Loreto are good bases for day trips to Magdalena Bay.

Blue whales

The largest animal that has ever lived feeds in the Sea of Cortez in late winter. Loreto's national marine park is one of the most reliable places in the world to see blue whales, usually from small pangas run by local guides. Calm mornings give the best chances.

Whale sharks

Whale sharks are gentle, plankton-eating sharks that can grow longer than a bus. Between June and September, they gather off the north-east tip of the Yucatán Peninsula to feed. Tours leave early from Holbox, Isla Mujeres and Cancún, and you snorkel alongside them in small groups. In the Bay of La Paz, younger whale sharks gather in the cooler months, with strict limits on boats and swimmers.

Choosing a responsible tour

Mexico regulates whale watching with rules on how close boats may approach, how long they may stay and how many boats can be near an animal at once. Whale-shark swimming has its own limits on swimmers per boat. The best operators follow them happily; the worst chase animals for photos. Look for:

  • A visible government permit and a licensed local guide.
  • Small groups, life jackets for everyone and a safety briefing.
  • Engines off or idling near animals, and no chasing.
  • No touching, feeding or flash photography, ever.
  • A clear plan for cancellations: weather and visibility decide everything.
